## Ranking by likely importance

1. **[ROMA: Recursive Open Meta-Agent Framework for Long-Horizon Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01848)**
2. **[When Single-Agent with Skills Replace Multi-Agent Systems and When They Fail](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04748)**
3. **[EmCoop: A Framework and Benchmark for Embodied Cooperation Among LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00349)**
4. **[The Vision Wormhole: Latent-Space Communication in Heterogeneous Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15382)**
5. **[Intelligent AI Delegation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11865)**

## Short version

My ranking is mostly driven by a simple question: **which of these is most likely to change how future multi-agent systems are actually built and evaluated?**

- **ROMA** ranks first because it proposes a fairly general systems architecture for long-horizon multi-agent work and backs it with meaningful benchmark gains.
- **Single-agent-with-skills vs multi-agent** ranks second because it attacks a foundational assumption of the area: that multi-agent structure is inherently necessary. Even if its concrete system is less influential than ROMA, its framing could age very well.
- **EmCoop** ranks third because benchmarks can have long half-lives, especially when they expose a real gap in evaluation.
- **Vision Wormhole** is more speculative: very novel, but also more contingent on whether latent-channel communication actually becomes practical and robust.
- **Intelligent AI Delegation** feels the least likely to be a lasting citation anchor because it appears more conceptual/framework-oriented than technically decisive.

## Why ROMA is #1

**Novelty**
- ROMA’s main idea is not just “use multiple agents,” but to make multi-agent orchestration **recursive, modular, and explicitly structured** around decomposition and aggregation. The paper says it introduces “recursive task decomposition and structured aggregation” and a four-role architecture of Atomizer, Planner, Executor, and Aggregator. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2602.01848" title="ROMA and GEPA+" page="1" first="Recursive task decomposition" last="to ROMA’s modular architecture." />
- That is a stronger contribution than a narrow tactic because it is an **architectural pattern** others can reuse.

**Practical impact**
- The strongest reason to put it first is that it claims concrete gains on recognizable long-horizon tasks. The paper evaluates ROMA on SEAL-0, EQ-Bench, FRAMES, SimpleQA, and AbGen. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2602.01848" title="Benchmarks" page="3" first="We evaluate ROMA" last="and AbGen [58 ]." />
- On SEAL-0, it reports **45.9% accuracy**, described as a **9.9 point improvement over Kimi-Researcher**. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2602.01848" title="SEAL-0" page="8" first="ROMA achieves the" last="open research agent." />
- On EQ-Bench, it claims ROMA plus GEPA+ lets DeepSeek-V3 reach **79.8%**, matching leading closed models. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2602.01848" title="EQ-Bench" page="9" first="Applying GEPA+ prompt" last="4.5 (see Table 4)." />

**Likely lasting relevance**
- The lasting part is that ROMA is trying to solve a durable systems problem: **how to scale reasoning depth without collapsing under context growth and orchestration brittleness**.
- Even if the exact role names do not persist, the decomposition/aggregation pattern probably will.
- The limitation section actually makes the paper more credible: it explicitly says decomposition and aggregation are not guaranteed optimal, aggregation quality depends on prompting and model behavior, and parallel execution can raise compute cost and coordination issues. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2602.01848" title="Limitations" page="13" first="While ROMA provides" last="intermediate results." /> <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2602.01848" title="Limitations" page="13" first="Though parallel execution" last="concurrent branches." />

> “While ROMA provides a” … “may omit critical evidence or over-compress intermediate results.” <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2602.01848" title="Limitations" page="13" first="While ROMA provides" last="intermediate results." />

**Why not lower?**
- Because it is both **broadly reusable** and **empirically grounded**, which is the best combination for importance.

## Why the single-agent-with-skills paper is #2

**Novelty**
- This paper asks a deeper question than “how do we build a better multi-agent system?” It asks whether many such systems should be compiled away into a **single agent with a skill library**.
- That is a high-value conceptual move because it challenges a lot of current multi-agent enthusiasm at the level of first principles. The paper frames skills as “internalized agent behaviors” and treats multi-agent systems as compilable into single-agent systems through skill selection. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2601.04748v2" title="Method" page="1" first="Multi-agent AI systems" last="skill selection." />

**Practical impact**
- It reports large efficiency gains: **53.7% average token reduction** and **49.5% average latency reduction** while maintaining similar benchmark accuracy. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2601.04748v2" title="Results" page="7" first="Faithful Compilation." last="MAS performance." />
- If those results hold broadly, this matters a lot in practice because much of multi-agent engineering is expensive and slow.

**Likely lasting relevance**
- I think this paper has very strong odds of being remembered because it identifies a likely **scaling law / failure mode** for skill routing: performance stays stable and then degrades sharply beyond a critical library size, with semantic confusability playing a major role. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2601.04748v2" title="Method" page="1" first="Rather than degrading gradually," last="human cognition." />
- That is the kind of finding that can outlive the exact experiments. It gives the field a vocabulary for when multi-agent decomposition is useful and when internalized skills stop scaling cleanly.

> “Rather than degrading gradually,” … “phase transition reminiscent of capacity limits in human cognition.” <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2601.04748v2" title="Method" page="1" first="Rather than degrading gradually," last="human cognition." />

**Why not #1?**
- Because the paper is more likely to be influential as a **critique and reframing** than as the dominant production architecture.
- ROMA feels more immediately adoptable as a build pattern; this one feels more like the paper people cite when deciding whether they need multi-agent structure at all.

## Why EmCoop is #3

**Novelty**
- EmCoop is not primarily an orchestration method. Its contribution is a **benchmark and analysis framework** for embodied cooperation among LLM agents.
- That matters because embodied multi-agent work badly needs better process-level evaluation, not just win rates.

**Practical impact**
- In the short term, benchmarks usually matter less than a strong system paper unless the benchmark becomes the community standard.
- But EmCoop’s emphasis on separating high-level cognition from low-level embodied interaction, plus process metrics for diagnosing cooperation quality and failure modes, is exactly the kind of infrastructure the area needs.

**Likely lasting relevance**
- Benchmarks can become very durable if they define the way a subfield measures progress.
- I ranked it above Vision Wormhole because evaluation frameworks often outlast clever communication mechanisms.
- If embodied multi-agent systems become a serious frontier, EmCoop could end up being one of the early reference points for **how to measure cooperation rather than just whether the task was solved**.

**Why not higher?**
- Because benchmark papers only become truly central if the community adopts them. That is plausible here, but not yet something I would bet on more strongly than the two papers above.

## Why Vision Wormhole is #4

**Novelty**
- This is probably the most **flashily novel** paper in the set. The idea of using a VLM visual interface as a universal latent communication channel for heterogeneous agents is unusual and memorable.
- The hub-and-spoke alignment idea, reducing pairwise alignment complexity from $O(N^2)$ to $O(N)$, is also structurally appealing.

**Practical impact**
- The paper claims reduced wall-clock time while preserving reasoning fidelity in controlled comparisons, which is directionally important.
- But this kind of contribution depends heavily on whether the latent channel is robust across models, tasks, updates, and deployment settings.

**Likely lasting relevance**
- It could become very important if future multi-agent systems move away from text-only interfaces and toward **high-bandwidth hidden-state exchange**.
- But it could also end up as a clever branch that does not become mainstream if text-mediated protocols remain easier to debug, safer, and more interoperable.
- So I see it as **high novelty, medium confidence**.

**Why below EmCoop?**
- Because benchmark infrastructure usually has a more stable path to lasting influence than a speculative communication mechanism that may be overtaken by other latent-routing or protocol-learning approaches.

---

## Why Intelligent AI Delegation is #5

**Novelty**
- The paper appears to propose a broad adaptive delegation framework involving authority, accountability, trust, and role boundaries.
- Those are important ideas, but from the evidence here it looks more like a **conceptual and governance-oriented framework** than a sharp technical breakthrough.

**Practical impact**
- It may influence design language and safety-oriented protocol thinking, especially in human-AI delegation settings.
- But papers of this type often matter more as agenda-setting documents than as core technical references that future systems directly build on.

**Likely lasting relevance**
- It could age well if “agentic web” coordination becomes a major area and this paper gets credited for articulating the delegation problem clearly.
- Still, compared with the others, it seems least likely to be the paper people repeatedly cite for a concrete mechanism, benchmark, or empirical result.

**Why last?**
- Because importance in this area tends to come from one of three things:
  - a reusable architecture,
  - a strong empirical reframing,
  - or a benchmark everyone adopts.
- This paper, at least from the available evidence, seems less decisive on those dimensions.

## The key tradeoff behind the ranking

A compact way to think about it:

- **ROMA**: best mix of method + evidence + generality.
- **Single-agent with skills**: most important challenge to the field’s assumptions.
- **EmCoop**: strongest infrastructure/evaluation contribution.
- **Vision Wormhole**: most daring mechanism, but higher risk of not becoming standard.
- **Intelligent AI Delegation**: important theme, weaker evidence of becoming a core technical landmark.
