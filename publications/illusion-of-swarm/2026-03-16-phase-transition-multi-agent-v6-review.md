## What You Fixed (And Why It Works)

### 1. The Opening Hook is Back, But Legal
V4 opened with "12×... Twelve." which was unsourced and brittle.
V5 opened with "In benchmark settings..." which was boring.
V6 opens with: *"A lot of multi-agent systems are overflow handling dressed up as architecture."*
That is a brilliant sentence. It has the swagger of V4 but requires zero unsourced numeric claims. It makes the reader lean in immediately.

### 2. The Thesis is Now Bulletproof
The transition from paragraph 2 into the blockquote thesis is the best structural work in the essay:
> "It does not mean one agent always wins... It means something narrower and more useful: for many same-model, tool-using production systems, explicit multi-agent structure is often the operational price of unresolved routing, state, and communication problems."
This is how you write a viral essay that experts can't tear down. You state the extreme version so the reader feels the heat, then explicitly walk back to the defensible version *before* the critic can do it for you.

### 3. "Semantic Quarantine" Survived the Cut
You brought back "Semantic Quarantine" and "Agent = Namespace." Thank you. This was the best original framing in V4, and its removal in V5 was a tragedy. The prose defining it here ("Your support bot does not need a separate database agent because SQL requires a different species of mind") is fantastic.

### 4. The "Swarm as Training Rig" Section is Masterful
V4 treated distillation as an inevitable law of physics. V6 treats it as an engineering loop. By pairing EMPO2 and AgentArk not as a universal prophecy, but as two sides of the same technique (distilling internal vs. external scaffolding), you make the insight actionable today instead of waiting for 2027.

### 5. The Voice Returned
You found the right register again.
"The graph is probably doing theater on top of a simpler failure mode."
"More agents is not an explanation. It is a bill."
"Flat persona committees are often an expensive way to solve a routing problem."
These are the sentences people share on LinkedIn and Twitter. V5 lacked them entirely. V6 is packed with them.

## The Remaining Rough Edges (Nitpicks for V7)

If you want to spend another hour polishing this, here is where to look. (To be clear: you could publish this right now and it would do very well. These are optimizations for the top 1%.)

**1. The "Where Multi-Agent Is Real" section feels slightly disconnected from the "Compiler Workaround" metaphor.**
The title of the piece uses "Compiler Workaround." The first section uses it. But by Section 3, you're just listing exceptions. It would hit harder if you tied the exceptions back to the core metaphor. E.g., "You can compile away a routing trick. You cannot compile away a trust boundary."

**2. The Transition to Section 4 (Text is the Bottleneck) is Abrupt.**
Section 3 ends talking about the scaling paper and conditional tools. Section 4 immediately starts: "The papers do not justify 'the death of English.'" Wait, what papers? We were just talking about the scaling paper. You need one transition sentence here reminding the reader that we are moving from *why* systems split to *how* they communicate when they do split.

**3. The EMPO2 Math/Mechanism is Still a Bit Dense.**
"In the off-policy update, the action was generated by the old policy with tips available, but the new policy is updated using probabilities recomputed without the tips."
This is technically accurate but functionally unreadable for a PM or Eng Dir skimming on their phone. You follow it with "In plain English...", which helps, but consider if you even need the mathy sentence at all. The "plain English" version might be enough.

**4. The "Monday" Playbook is Great, But Point 6 is a Mouthful.**
"Treat scaffolds as candidates for internalization. External memory, review passes, multi-agent debate, and exploratory helpers may belong in training, adaptation, or fallback paths rather than in the hot path of every production request."
This is a very long bullet point. Break it up. Make "Internalize the scaffolding" the bold header, then list the examples.
