## Promise

This essay will show that the most important limit on AI delegation is not whether systems help in the moment, but whether the workflow preserves corrective competence: the human ability to detect, contest, and repair errors once assistance becomes normal. This frame survives pressure because the core evidence seam now spans causal impairment, skill formation, competence-preserving intervention, expert-domain failure proxies, and formal workflow analysis. The remaining risk is not lack of a concept. It is overgeneralizing too quickly across domains without naming which mechanisms actually travel.

## Spine

1. Open with the persistence result from `2604.04721` and make the paradox visible immediately.
2. Define corrective competence and distinguish it from trust, calibration, and generic understanding.
3. Build the mechanism layer from skill formation, epistemic debt, and competence shadow.
4. Handle the calibration / appropriate-reliance rival in one bounded hinge section.
5. End with a workflow doctrine about what delegation-safe systems must preserve.

## Paragraph burden

- Opening: establish one unforgettable anomaly, not a survey preamble.
- Definition: make `corrective competence` feel exact rather than fashionable.
- Mechanism: show why short-term helpfulness can coexist with weaker later oversight.
- Rival hinge: concede what calibration explains without letting it absorb the essay.
- Ending: convert the synthesis into a practical evaluation rule for delegation.

## Failure triggers

- `Corrective competence` becomes a catch-all label for any collaboration problem.
- The calibration rival expands into a co-equal essay.
- The middle uses the expert, novice, and safety literatures as if they were interchangeable without argument.
- The ending collapses into generic governance language.
