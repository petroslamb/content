# Corrective Competence Blueprint

## Active Thesis

The deeper failure mode in human-AI collaboration is not just that systems are wrong or that people rely on them badly. It is that some forms of AI assistance weaken the user's corrective competence: the practical ability to notice, contest, and repair what has gone wrong. A system can raise assisted output while degrading independent performance, narrowing hypothesis generation, and hollowing out oversight. That is a stronger public object than a narrower essay about benchmark realism or calibration because it names the thing operators actually lose when delegation goes bad.

This frame survives pressure because the evidence seam no longer depends on one type of paper or one domain. The package now has: causal evidence that short AI exposure can reduce persistence and later independent performance; skill-formation evidence that some AI use patterns preserve learning while others erode it; intervention papers that try to reduce epistemic debt rather than just improve output; expert-domain behavioral proxies for automation bias, confirmation bias, and inappropriate directional change; and a safety-governance theory that formalizes hidden competence loss as a workflow problem rather than a pure capability problem.

## Governing Term

Use `corrective competence` as the public governing term.

Reasons:

- It foregrounds active oversight capacity rather than passive understanding.
- It travels better in public prose than `capability-comprehension gap`, which remains useful as an internal technical descriptor.
- It fits the evidence seam cleanly: persistence loss, weaker independent debugging, automation bias, confirmation bias, and shadowed hypothesis generation are all failures of correction, not just failures of comprehension.

Internal support term:

- `capability-comprehension gap`
  Use only when the essay needs to describe the structural divergence between assisted output and retained understanding.

## Section-Level Argument Spine

### Section 1. The Short-Term Win That Weakens You

Job:

- Open on the persistence finding from `2604.04721`.
- Establish the paradox: AI can improve immediate performance while degrading what the person can do once the system disappears.
- Make the reader feel the stakes before introducing the term.

### Section 2. What Corrective Competence Is

Job:

- Define corrective competence as the ability to detect, contest, and repair errors under assistance.
- Distinguish it from raw accuracy, generic trust, and simple calibration.
- Introduce `capability-comprehension gap` only as supporting technical language if needed.

### Section 3. Why Better Output Can Produce Worse Oversight

Job:

- Show that the problem is not just wrong answers.
- Use skill-formation, epistemic-debt, and competence-shadow papers to explain how assistance can narrow learning, debugging, persistence, and hypothesis generation.
- Make explicit that this is a workflow-governance problem, not a simple procurement or UX problem.

### Section 4. The Rival Explanation Is Real But Smaller

Job:

- Address calibration and appropriate reliance briefly but directly.
- Use the clinical expert papers to show that inappropriate reliance cuts both ways: blind acceptance and persistent under-reliance are both real.
- Argue that these are not alternative explanations that displace corrective competence. They are surface manifestations inside the same deeper oversight problem.

### Section 5. What A Safer Workflow Must Preserve

Job:

- Bring in contestability-preserving workflow design as the strongest supporting seam.
- Specify what systems must preserve: independent first-pass judgment where needed, evidence visibility, challengeability, teach-back or explanation gating where appropriate, and human checkpoints that test retained competence rather than ceremonial signoff.
- End with an operator doctrine: do not ask whether the AI helps; ask whether the workflow preserves enough corrective competence to let people intervene well.

## Rival Frame And Borders

### Main rival frame

- `appropriate reliance engineering`

The essay must concede:

- calibration matters
- under-reliance is real
- interface and evidence presentation change behavior

The essay must not concede:

- that better calibration alone solves the problem
- that corrective competence is merely a different name for trust calibration
- that the right question is only whether teams outperform unaided humans in the moment

### Non-claims

- This is not a generic anti-AI thesis.
- This is not a claim that assistance always reduces competence.
- This is not a claim that users need full independent replication of every task.
- This is not a claim that contestability mechanisms automatically preserve competence.
- This is not a claim that expert under-reliance disproves the deeper frame.

## Failure Triggers

Abort or reset the draft if any of these happen:

- The essay turns into a benchmark-evaluation sequel instead of a new public object.
- `corrective competence` is defined so loosely that it becomes a synonym for any collaboration problem.
- The rival calibration frame occupies a co-equal body share and recenters the essay around trust rather than correction.
- The piece relies on abstract theory before earning the stakes with a concrete opening exhibit.
- The ending drifts into generic governance language without a specific delegation rule.

### Promise

This essay will show that the most important limit on AI delegation is not whether systems help in the moment, but whether they preserve the human capacity to catch, challenge, and repair errors once assistance becomes normal.

### Spine

1. Opening anomaly: short-term help, weaker independent performance.
2. Definition: what corrective competence is and is not.
3. Mechanism: how assistance can erode persistence, learning, debugging, and hypothesis generation.
4. Counterpressure: why calibration and appropriate reliance matter but do not absorb the whole phenomenon.
5. Doctrine: what a delegation-safe workflow must preserve.

### Paragraph Burden

- Opening: force the paradox into public view with one vivid study result.
- Definition section: make the term usable in one pass without jargon pileup.
- Mechanism section: prove the phenomenon is structural, not anecdotal.
- Counterpressure section: answer the best rival without letting it become the essay.
- Doctrine ending: turn the synthesis into a concrete rule for deployment and workflow design.
