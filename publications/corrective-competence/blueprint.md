# Corrective Competence Blueprint

## North-Star Thesis

The central failure mode in many human-AI workflows is not just inaccuracy. It is loss of corrective competence: systems can improve assisted output while simultaneously weakening the user's ability to detect, contest, and repair errors. That is the deeper limit on safe delegation and the stronger editorial object than a narrower argument about benchmark miscalibration.

## Why This Object Cleared

- The donor package exhausted the benchmark-interpretation frame and documented the ceiling.
- The post-ceiling pivot process cleared corrective competence / capability-comprehension gap as the strongest flagship successor.
- The evidence seam now includes theory, bounded empirical support, behavioral proxy papers, and supporting workflow-governance material.
- Rival frames such as calibration-only or appropriate-reliance-only explanations remain important but do not absorb the whole seam.

## Non-Claims

- This is not a generic claim that AI assistance always degrades judgment.
- This is not a claim that all human oversight is fake or doomed.
- This is not a claim that calibration and interface design do not matter.
- This is not a claim that every human-AI workflow requires full independent replication.

## Section Spine

1. Open with a concrete case where assisted performance rises while independent corrective capacity degrades.
2. Define corrective competence and distinguish it from accuracy, reliance, and simple trust calibration.
3. Show why capability gains can coexist with worse intervention quality.
4. Use supporting seams on contestability-preserving workflow design to show how systems can preserve or erode corrective competence.
5. End with an operator doctrine about what workflows may safely delegate and what evidence they need before doing so.
