## Hopfield Networks & Transformer Attention Equivalence

[Hopfield Networks is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217) (5 years ago) from Johannes Kepler University and collaborators introduces modern Hopfield networks with continuous states that can store exponentially many patterns, establishing the mathematical foundation connecting Hopfield networks to transformer attention mechanisms.

[Understanding Transformer from the Perspective of Associative Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19488) (8 months ago) provides reflections on understanding transformer architectures through the lens of associative memory, connecting classical psychological concepts to modern attention mechanisms.

[Test-time regression: a unifying framework for designing sequence models with associative memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12352) (a year ago) from Google DeepMind and Stanford unifies seemingly unrelated architectures like transformers and recurrent models through the lens of associative memory and test-time regression.

[Modern Methods in Associative Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06211) (7 months ago) from IBM Research and collaborators surveys recent developments in associative memory models including modern Hopfield networks and their connections to transformer architectures.

[Understanding Factual Recall in Transformers via Associative Memories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06538) (a year ago) from Princeton and Flatiron Institute analyzes how transformers perform factual recall by modeling them as associative memory systems, finding they can store information at rates proportional to their capacity.

## Key-Value Memory & Feed-Forward Layers

[Transformer Feed-Forward Layers Are Key-Value Memories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14913) (4 years ago) from Tel Aviv and Allen AI demonstrates that feed-forward layers in transformers function as key-value memories, constituting two-thirds of model parameters yet remaining under-explored.

[Key-value memory in the brain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02950) (a year ago) from Harvard examines key-value memory mechanisms from a neuroscience perspective, contrasting them with classical similarity-based retrieval in psychological and neuroscience models.

[It's All Connected: A Journey Through Test-Time Memorization, Attentional Bias, Retention, and Online Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13173) (10 months ago) from Google Research explores content-addressable memory mechanisms inspired by human cognitive phenomena of attentional bias and memory retention.

[Blending Complementary Memory Systems in Hybrid Quadratic-Linear Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00744) (8 months ago) from Harvard and MIT develops hybrid memory architectures combining key-value memory using softmax attention with fast weight memory through dynamic updates.

## State Space Models: Retrieval & Needle in Haystack

[Transformers are SSMs: Generalized Models and Efficient Algorithms Through Structured State Space Duality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.21060) (2 years ago) from CMU and Princeton establishes the duality between transformers and state space models like Mamba, showing their mathematical equivalence under certain conditions.

[NoLiMa: Long-Context Evaluation Beyond Literal Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167) (a year ago) from LMU Munich and Adobe provides a comprehensive evaluation framework for long-context capabilities beyond the simple needle-in-a-haystack test, addressing limitations of literal matching approaches.

[In Search of Needles in a 11M Haystack: Recurrent Memory Finds What LLMs Miss](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10790) (2 years ago) from AIRI introduces BABILong benchmark to assess long document processing, showing recurrent memory architectures can find information that standard LLMs miss in ultra-long contexts.

[LongMamba: Enhancing Mamba's Long Context Capabilities via Training-Free Receptive Field Enlargement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16053) (9 months ago) from Georgia Tech and NVIDIA addresses state space models' limited receptive fields through training-free methods to enhance long-context capabilities.

[Understanding the Skill Gap in Recurrent Language Models: The Role of the Gather-and-Aggregate Mechanism](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18574) (9 months ago) from CMU analyzes why state space models struggle with algorithmic tasks like retrieving past context, identifying the gather-and-aggregate mechanism as key to understanding their limitations.

[Mechanistic evaluation of Transformers and state space models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15105) (8 months ago) from Stanford compares SSMs and transformers on recalling basic information from context, revealing variable performance on memory-intensive retrieval tasks.

## Induction Heads & In-Context Learning

[In-context Learning and Induction Heads](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11895) (3 years ago) from Anthropic presents foundational evidence that induction heads—attention heads implementing simple copying algorithms like [A][B]...[A]→[B]—are critical for in-context learning capabilities.

[Learning without training: The implicit dynamics of in-context learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16003) (6 months ago) from Google Research examines how LLMs learn in-context at inference time without weight updates, analyzing the implicit dynamics underlying pattern learning.

[The Dual-Route Model of Induction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03022) (10 months ago) from Northeastern discovers a new type of induction head beyond individual token copying: concept-level induction heads that operate on higher-level semantic patterns.

[Beyond Induction Heads: In-Context Meta Learning Induces Multi-Phase Circuit Emergence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16694) (8 months ago) from University of Tokyo shows that in-context learning involves more than just induction heads, revealing multi-phase circuit emergence during meta-learning.

[Which Attention Heads Matter for In-Context Learning?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14010) (a year ago) from UC Berkeley investigates which specific attention mechanisms drive ICL, comparing different proposed mechanisms including induction heads.

[Induction Heads as an Essential Mechanism for Pattern Matching in In-context Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07011) (a year ago) from University of Amsterdam provides comprehensive analysis of induction heads as the essential mechanism for pattern matching in ICL tasks.

[Retrieval Head Mechanistically Explains Long-Context Factuality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15574) (2 years ago) from University of Washington and collaborators identifies "retrieval heads" that mechanistically explain how transformers retrieve information from arbitrary locations in long contexts.

## Additional Foundational Works

[Bridging Associative Memory and Probabilistic Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10202) (2 years ago) from Stanford and Meta connects associative memory mechanisms with probabilistic modeling approaches, showing deep relationships between these frameworks.

Your research interests span the fundamental dynamics and emergent properties of deep neural networks, particularly transformers. I've curated a list of the most relevant papers covering grokking, scaling laws, and structural properties like the lottery ticket hypothesis.

### Grokking and Generalization Dynamics

[Grokking: Generalization Beyond Overfitting on Small Algorithmic Datasets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02177) (4 years ago) from Google and OpenAI is the seminal work that first characterized "grokking"—a phenomenon where models achieve near-perfect generalization long after overfitting to training data.

[Progress measures for grokking via mechanistic interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217) (3 years ago) from UC Berkeley uses mechanistic interpretability to reveal that grokking involves a transition from memorizing circuits to generalizing circuits, providing "progress measures" that predict when grokking will occur.

[Let Me Grok for You: Accelerating Grokking via Embedding Transfer from a Weaker Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13292) (10 months ago) from UC Berkeley and University of Michigan explores how transferring internal representations from smaller models can significantly speed up the grokking process in larger networks.

[The Complexity Dynamics of Grokking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09810) (a year ago) from the University of Oxford demonstrates a complexity phase transition during grokking, where the model's internal representations simplify as it moves from memorization to generalization.

[Bridging Lottery Ticket and Grokking: Understanding Grokking from Inner Structure of Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19470) (2 years ago) from the University of Tokyo connects these two major concepts, suggesting that grokking can be understood through the emergence of specific sparse sub-networks (winning tickets) within the larger model.

### Scaling Laws and Emergent Capabilities

[Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361) (6 years ago) from OpenAI provides the foundational power laws relating model performance to parameter count, dataset size, and compute, which have since guided the development of massive LLMs.

[Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07682) (3 years ago) from Google and Stanford identifies "emergent" abilities—tasks that models cannot perform at small scales but suddenly master once a certain scale (the "phase transition") is reached.

[Triple Phase Transitions: Understanding the Learning Dynamics of Large Language Models from a Neuroscience Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20779) (a year ago) from Osaka University and collaborators identifies three distinct phases during LLM training, providing a biological and mechanical framework for understanding emergent behavior.

[Compute Optimal Scaling of Skills: Knowledge vs Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10061) (a year ago) from Meta and the University of Wisconsin analyzes how the scaling laws for "knowledge" (memorization) differ from those for "reasoning" (generalization), suggesting distinct compute-optimal paths for different model capabilities.

### Lottery Ticket Hypothesis and Structural Properties

[The Lottery Ticket Hypothesis: Finding Sparse, Trainable Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03635) (7 years ago) is the original paper proposing that dense networks contain sparse sub-networks ("winning tickets") that, when trained in isolation, can match the performance of the full network.

[Weight-sparse transformers have interpretable circuits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13653) (3 months ago) from OpenAI shows that inducing sparsity in transformers leads to more interpretable internal "circuits," suggesting that the lottery ticket effect is linked to the mechanistic clarity of the model's logic.

[The Strong Lottery Ticket Hypothesis for Multi-Head Attention Mechanisms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04217) (3 months ago) specifically examines how the "strong" version of the hypothesis applies to attention mechanisms, proving that high-performing sub-networks are inherent in randomly initialized attention layers.

### Optimization Stability and Training Dynamics

[On Layer Normalization in the Transformer Architecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04745) (6 years ago) provides a critical analysis of where to place normalization layers to ensure training stability and eliminate the need for delicate learning rate warm-up schedules.

[Small-scale proxies for large-scale Transformer training instabilities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322) (2 years ago) from Google DeepMind investigates how to predict and mitigate training instabilities that only emerge at massive scales by using carefully designed small-scale proxy experiments.

[A Unified View of Attention and Residual Sinks: Outlier-Driven Rescaling is Essential for Transformer Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22966) (5 days ago) explores the role of "outliers" (attention and residual sinks) in maintaining training stability in transformers, providing a new perspective on signal propagation.

These papers provide a comprehensive view of how scale, sparsity, and specific training phases dictate the performance and internal logic of modern neural networks.
