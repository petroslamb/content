If the need is mainly senior implementation for a pre-decided platform, it is better for us to know that now.

How I see ownership:

Pere owns the community and product bet. That means who the sessions and broader expert-community product serve, what experts get back, what sessions matter, what content or insight should come out of them, and what success looks like from the community and business side.

In the role I am describing, I would be accountable for turning that bet into a technical operating system. That means discovery, architecture, data model, AI workflow, build-versus-buy, integration plan, the first operator prototype, and the technical metrics that show whether the session-driven system is compounding. Michael, or another named sponsor, owns the company support layer. That means the technical bar, access to systems and data, sponsorship, core-IP judgment, and evaluation of whether the first phase is strong enough to continue. Compliance needs to own consent and reuse boundaries. Transcript reuse, inferred expertise, anonymization, retention, AI processing, and client-facing outputs should not become informal engineering risk.

What this role is, and what it is not:

This role is technical ownership of the operating loop. It is not just building screens, APIs, dashboards, and integrations after the platform has already been defined.

This role is discovery and architecture before platform commitment. It is not starting a platform build while the data, consent, workflow, and success metrics are still unclear.

This role is proving whether the Delphi session format can support a repeatable expert-community system around it. It is not measuring success by whether a portal or feature list ships.

The main points I would like us to resolve are: whether the first phase is discovery/prototype rather than platform delivery, who sponsors and evaluates the technical work, what access and compliance support would exist, and what decision rights come with the role.
