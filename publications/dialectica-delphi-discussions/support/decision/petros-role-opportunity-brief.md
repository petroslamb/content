# Petros Role Opportunity Brief

Working brief for evaluating the proposed role: Pere leads the product/community side of the Delphi expert-community idea, while Petros leads the technical side and turns the concept into an executable system.

## Role Thesis

The strongest version of this role is not "senior engineer for a community platform." That framing is too small. The real work is to become the technical owner of an expert-community operating system.

Pere has the product problem: Delphi needs to become more than a set of Zooms. It needs a way to convene experts, keep them engaged, turn their participation into value, and convert the resulting insight into useful content, client intelligence, and network effects. The technical role is to design the machinery that makes this repeatable.

That means the job is closer to technical product architecture than ordinary feature delivery. It includes data modeling, workflow design, AI-assisted synthesis, email and journey orchestration, expert identity, consent boundaries, content operations, and analytics. The platform only matters if it supports that operating loop.

## Why The Opportunity Is Interesting

Dialectica already has the hard part many community startups lack: an existing expert pool, real client demand, and a business model built around expert access. Delphi adds a different asset: observed expert reasoning in live discussions.

The opportunity is to connect those layers. A normal expert network knows who an expert is and can route them into paid calls. A Delphi-style network can learn how experts reason, where they disagree, what topics pull them back into the room, and which conversations create reusable insight.

If that data becomes structured, the technical system can start compounding. Each session enriches profiles. Each profile improves invitations. Each invitation improves the room. Each room generates better content. Each content artifact creates a reason for the next expert to participate.

## Michael As Technical Context

Michael Petychakis should be treated as a key sponsor/context source, not just a participant in the AI ROI material. He is Dialectica's CTO, Petros's initial contact, and the connector to Pere. That means his AI ROI comments are useful evidence for how the company may evaluate technical leadership: not by how much software gets built, but by whether the work improves operating leverage.

The AI ROI session suggests that Michael is sensitive to the difference between AI-enabled activity and actual business productivity. For this role, that is valuable. It gives Petros a way to speak in company-native terms: baseline the current Delphi workflow, identify process bottlenecks, prototype the smallest loop, measure adoption and operator time, and build only where Dialectica has a proprietary advantage.

The practical implication is that Petros should align with both Pere and Michael. Pere can define the community and content ambition. Michael can clarify the technical bar, engineering integration path, data access, buy-versus-build posture, and what counts as ROI for a new internal platform.

## The Role Boundary To Ask For

The role should be framed around ownership of technical discovery and architecture for the Delphi expert-community system.

That should include authority over:

- how the expert journey is modeled
- what data needs to be captured from Zoom, chat, transcripts, email, and profiles
- how the "give/get" engagement engine works
- what should be bought, integrated, or built
- how AI is used for summaries, claim maps, routing, and follow-up
- what metrics define whether the community is compounding
- what compliance and consent constraints shape the system

The role should not be limited to implementing tickets for a pre-decided platform. If the architecture is wrong, the community becomes a content calendar with a signup form. The senior technical contribution is making sure the product has a real operating spine.

## The Four Systems Hidden Inside The Idea

The Delphi concept bundles four systems that need to be separated before they can be built.

First, there is the community system: invitations, cohorts, attendance, expert circles, peer trust, introductions, and return loops.

Second, there is the content system: transcripts, chat signals, claim extraction, anonymized summaries, executive briefs, public-safe teasers, and client-facing intelligence.

Third, there is the data system: expert attributes, topics, contributions, consent state, session history, follow-up behavior, engagement scores, and inferred expertise.

Fourth, there is the workflow system: dbt-style modeling, rule or prompt evaluation, email triggers, operator review, feedback capture, and next-best-action logic.

The job should be to make these systems coherent. If they are collapsed into one generic "platform," the project will drift.

## Strong Initial Point Of View

The first version should probably not be a standalone community platform. It should be a thin operating layer around the tools Dialectica already uses.

The minimum useful system is likely:

- a structured expert/session data model
- a transcript and chat ingestion workflow
- a way to generate anonymized session artifacts
- a give/get offer library
- rules or prompts that recommend the next expert action
- email templates and review workflows
- a basic dashboard for Pere and operators

This would let the team test whether Delphi produces compounding behavior before investing in a heavy member-facing product.

## Personal Positioning

The strongest positioning for Petros is:

"I can lead the technical discovery and architecture for Delphi as a live expert-network system. My focus would be turning the Zoom community, expert data, content workflows, and give/get engagement model into an operating platform that can be tested quickly and scaled carefully."

That framing does three useful things. It treats Pere as the product/community lead. It gives the technical role strategic ownership. It avoids prematurely promising a large platform before the actual system requirements are known.

## Risks To Surface Early

The role becomes risky if the company wants senior-level ambiguity solved with junior-level authority. The system touches data, compliance, product, community, content, and email. It will fail if the technical lead cannot inspect the real workflows and influence product scope.

The other risk is premature platform building. A beautiful member portal does not prove the expert network is compounding. The early proof should come from repeat attendance, expert contribution, intro acceptance, content usefulness, and client pull.

The final risk is expert trust. If experts feel that Delphi extracts unpaid intelligence without giving serious value back, the network will weaken. The technical system has to encode fairness, consent, and visible benefit into the engagement loop.

## First 30 Days

The first month should be discovery and thin prototype design, not full build.

Useful outputs:

- current-system map
- expert journey map
- source-data inventory
- compliance and consent map
- first give/get offer taxonomy
- session-to-artifact workflow
- MVP architecture sketch
- top metrics for community compounding

By the end of the first month, Pere and the technical lead should know whether they are building an event follow-up workflow, an expert engagement engine, a content intelligence system, or all three in sequence.
