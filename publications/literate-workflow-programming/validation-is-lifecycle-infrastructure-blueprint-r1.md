## Promise

This piece argues one doctrine claim, not the whole literate-workflow-programming flagship frame: once workflow specifications, policy surfaces, and evidence maps govern agent behavior, validation becomes lifecycle infrastructure rather than terminal QA. The essay should make one public mistake newly visible, show what breaks when that mistake persists, and use the local `spec-diff` proof as a bounded exhibit instead of as the entire argument.

## Spine

1. Open with the wrong-layer mistake, but immediately cash it out as a validation doctrine problem rather than a slogan.
2. Show the real mistake: governed workflow surfaces are being treated as advisory prose even while validators and policy checks already depend on them.
3. Establish the doctrine hinge: validation is what keeps source-like workflow surfaces attached to evidence, constraints, and allowed behavior across the lifecycle.
4. Use the trace/provenance split to show why runtime logs help only after source expectations already exist.
5. Close by stating the operational consequence: builders must treat validation as infrastructure, not as terminal QA or editorial cleanup.

## Paragraph burden

- Opening: make the reader feel the wrong-layer error without turning the essay into a wrapper-only piece.
- Mistake paragraph: define the advisory-prose illusion in one crisp move.

- Enforcement paragraph: show that policy and constraint checks already act on those surfaces as control points.
