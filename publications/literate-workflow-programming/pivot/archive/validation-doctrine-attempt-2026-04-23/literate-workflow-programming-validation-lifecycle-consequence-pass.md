## Public Enemy Rerank

The weakest version of the candidate says:

- validation should happen earlier

That is too small and too ordinary.

The stronger enemy visible across the package is:

- treating governed workflow surfaces as advisory prose

That enemy is larger than terminal QA. It names the actual architectural mistake:

- the specification stays human-readable, so operators keep treating it as commentary
- meanwhile validators, policy layers, and traces are already depending on it as a governing surface
- failures are then diagnosed in logs, outputs, or runtime code long after the source-level drift has already happened

This is why the `r3` wrapper survived. "You're debugging the wrong layer" is not only a line-level accusation. It is the public face of the candidate's stronger doctrinal claim.

## Consequence Surface

The current evidence supports four real downstream consequences.

### 1. Constraint drift becomes operational, not merely editorial

`ContextCov` is strong here. Its whole premise is that instruction-level constraints cannot remain informal once the context surface grows and the runtime starts relying on them. Natural-language constraints that stay advisory become violations that have to be intercepted structurally.

That makes the consequence sharper than "better documentation":

- drift in the workflow layer becomes runtime-visible policy or process breakage

### 2. Specification failures cascade through multi-stage systems

`AgentFixer` makes the consequence larger than one failed run. It frames failure detection, cross-stage checks, diagnosis, root-cause analysis, trace comparison, and remediation as one loop because isolated runtime debugging is too late once failures propagate across a workflow.

This is a stronger consequence than "continuous evaluation is good":

- if validation is terminal, architectural failures spread through the workflow before anyone has a coherent diagnosis surface

### 3. Policy surfaces are real control points, not just safety decoration

`ALARA` and `Progent` matter because they show that least-privilege context, tool access, and policy-backed runtime blocking are operating on file-backed or spec-backed control surfaces. That means the workflow layer is not merely descriptive. It is where authority boundaries are being declared and enforced.

The consequence is:

- if operators treat those surfaces as commentary, they will misread where permission, failure, and containment are actually determined

### 4. Trace-only systems fail when claims must remain auditable

`AgentPex`, `FASTRIC`, and especially `Claim-Level Auditability` sharpen the limit. Traces are valuable because they can be checked against declared rules. They are not enough when the failure has shifted from wrong outputs to wrong reasons, lost constraints, or unverifiable synthesis.

The consequence is:

- without validation and provenance in the lifecycle, post-hoc logs cannot recover whether a promoted claim was ever actually governed by the right constraints and evidence
