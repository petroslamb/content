## Promise

This pass keeps the same thesis and tries to solve the last identified blocker. The essay still argues that workflow specifications become source-like artifacts only when they are interpreted, validated, constrained, and debugged as governing surfaces. `r3` must now sustain the opening pressure instead of relaxing into orderly stack summary. The reader should feel throughout that the old instinct, to look only for hidden implementation and treat workflow files as notes, is not just incomplete but operationally wrong.

## Spine

1. Opening accusation: start from a concrete validator-caught drift failure and name the mistake of debugging only the hidden layer.
2. Antagonist definition: make the competing view explicit, that markdown workflow artifacts are still just notes and that the real system lives elsewhere.
3. Source-like layer under pressure: show why interpretable workflow surfaces are already governing behavior.
4. Validation and policy confrontation: show that validators and policy layers are what give that surface authority rather than aspiration.
5. Trace hierarchy and local proof: use `spec-diff` and trace evaluation to show why debugging the wrong layer produces the wrong diagnosis.
6. Bounded skills and provenance close: keep skills compressed, then land on provenance as the next missing layer and the real remaining gap.

## Paragraph burden

- The opening must accuse the old debugging instinct, not merely describe the system.
- The second movement must name the antagonist clearly enough that later sections can keep pressing against it.
- The source-like layer section must prove that the workflow surface governs behavior without drifting into markdown maximalism.
- The validation and trace sections must keep increasing pressure, not just expanding the stack.
- The final movement must make provenance feel like the next necessary demand, not a detached afterthought.
