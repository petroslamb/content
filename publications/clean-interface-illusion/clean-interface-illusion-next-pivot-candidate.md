## Working Thesis

AI assistance can raise visible performance while eroding the human capacity required for genuine oversight. The key variable is corrective competence: the ability to independently detect, interrogate, contest, and repair AI-assisted work without depending on the AI that produced it.

## Why This Candidate Cleared

The winning seam now has:

- explicit theoretical frameworks for capability-comprehension loss and minimum viable human understanding
- direct empirical evidence that AI assistance can improve output while degrading persistence, skill formation, or independent performance
- formal bounds on performance loss under AI assistance in safety engineering and related settings
- stronger adjacent workflow evidence showing which structures help preserve or fail to preserve the conditions under which corrective competence matters
- quantified behavioral proxies that operationalize the gap in expert settings: directional change against AI, normalized weight-of-advice, automation-bias rate, confirmation-bias coefficients, Brier score, and hypothesis-selection behavior
- rival intervention papers that improve calibration or verification but largely fail to eliminate the human-AI team gap

## Residual Risk

The strongest remaining risk is threshold and domain calibration.

It is the **calibration problem**:

- the seam still lacks domain-calibrated thresholds for how much preserved understanding is enough for genuine oversight
- longitudinal and cross-domain field evidence remains thinner than the theoretical case
- the relation between preserved competence and contestability-preserving structure still needs a cleaner integrated account

The strongest adjacent workflow seam now clarifies the environment in which this object matters:

- workflows that preserve independent judgment, provenance, and contestability help sustain the conditions under which corrective competence can operate
- workflows that black-box intermediate reasoning make competence erosion more dangerous

## Residual Gaps To Carry Into Blueprint

The most important non-blocking gaps are:

- domain-calibrated or practically actionable thresholds for minimum viable understanding
- stronger longitudinal field evidence outside the current dominant settings
- a cleaner synthesis showing how workflow-level contestability preserves or fails to preserve corrective competence
- sharper boundary conditions showing when the phenomenon is better explained by calibration failure alone rather than by competence loss
