## Honest Weaknesses

1. **IAIP-to-Transformer link is asserted, not derived.** Why does attention achieve trainability + scalability + GEMM-alignment better than convolution + pooling + skip connections? Token-mixing flexibility is implied but not demonstrated.

2. **No experimental evidence.** Would be stronger with ablation studies. Relies on others' observations without new data.

3. **Tier is self-assigned.** A skeptic: "This is retrospective just-so story. Where's the prediction that resolved?"

## Publication Strategy

### Recommended Approach

| Order | Venue | Purpose |
|-------|-------|---------|
| 1 | **ArXiv** | Citeability, signals seriousness, sharper writing |
| 2 | **Substack** | Accessible version, links to ArXiv, reaches practitioners |
| 3 | **Twitter/X** | Condensed thread pointing to ArXiv + Substack |
| 4 | **HN / Reddit** | If thread gets traction |

### Why ArXiv First

- Tier 3 requires citeability. ArXiv is minimal threshold for ML researchers to engage.
- 6-8 page "position paper" or "working note" is accepted format.
- Forces sharpened writing.
- Suggested category: `cs.LG` (Machine Learning)

### Substack Alone: Insufficient

- Not credentialed for academic adoption
- Tier 2 frameworks don't spread without controversy or novelty hook
- Substack readers want "what to do" more than "here's a framework"

## Bottom Line

The thesis is good work, but **dissertation-level synthesis, not paradigm-shifting insight**. At Tier 2, it's a useful map for people who don't want to read 50 papers. Not yet a tool that changes what people build.

Post on ArXiv first, then Substack. The real test is 2028. If predictions hold and IAIP discriminates, you'll have Tier 3. If an architecture breaks out that you didn't predict, the thesis becomes historical curiosity.

That's the honest stakes.
